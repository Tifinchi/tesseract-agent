# Tesseract Logic Calculator — the author's rules and the method of use

Source: *Tesseract Logic Calculator* (Tifinchi, Oct 2026; OSF DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/C9W7K;
github.com/Tifinchi/Tesseract-Logic-Calculator), sections 4.1, 4.8 and 4.9. The motor-and-circuit
calculator is one worked example of this method; the method is general.

## 4.1 The author's rules (quoted as written)

1. **Provenance.** A cube where "when we invert or rotate the cube we can keep track of exactly which component the new data originated from."
2. **The fixed cube.** "the cube itself doesnt move...inverses become tesseracts...sphere has pi euler inversion...anything in motion starts as a single vertice on the cube...rather than changing size of the cube for derivatives, we are switching to another layer of tesseract but the data points should still remain anchored in the process...same for dimension inversions...for the pois, there should already observed data that lines up in ever intansantanous and motion of the cipher."
3. **Time and scale.** "now you can also use this cipher applied as derivatives and inverses of time and scale without ever moving the cube."
4. **Folding, not reversing.** "in the t-bar flip time isnt reversed neg or inverse on the mag isn't reverse...the same as rt2 to rt4 causes higher resolution, it decreases in reverse. info isnt lost just folded"
5. **Neighbours.** "if components don't fit, we should be able to find them by their adjascent data to the vertice....meaning we should be able to sort out the correct direction, flow, scale, time, motion, distance, and resolution or literally anything in unverse despite unknown dynamics because those work in ranges across the full structure"
6. **The vertex lock.** "if a data point is a vertice...then the 3 side lengths and subsequent 3 data points from should also line up."
7. **The sphere.** Action rides the curve and inside is no action; the T-bar flip rotates between action (√2) and no action (√4).
8. **The reaction.** Put electrons "as a reaction in the fabric rather than a particle, and align the inductance and other components to their correct geometry."
9. **Behavior.** "anomalous BEHAVIOR plays the most key role in the calculator, because while a number set may be 'in range' the practical real effects of what the numbers produce in every alignment do not. So we use the alignments that do not create down-wind mismatched observeables." And: "We leave in success with proof, and leave out trials that failed."

## 4.8 The method

Anchor each part of a datum at a corner of the fixed cube, follow each moving part round its
corner's circle, and match every position on the circle to a measurement. A rate takes one
quarter turn and an inverse two.

1. **Write the cube.** Copy the eight delimiters and the six face strings exactly. Mark the tetrahedron {″, ., +, ×} and the dual corners.
2. **Choose the layer's frame.** Say what sits on the corners, faces or axes in this layer, and which axis is held. A tetrahedron layer puts four quantities on ″, ., + and ×; an axis layer puts three components on x, y and z. In spacetime layers, time is the held axis.
3. **Fix the corners from faces already named.** Two adjacent faces share exactly one tetrahedron corner, so three mutually adjacent named faces fix three corners, and the fourth is the one left.
4. **Anchor every datum.** Write which corner, face or axis carries each part. Data never leave their corners.
5. **Turn; do not move.** For a rate, take each part one quarter turn along its corner's circle; for the inverse, two; for the anti-rate, three. A mirror in three dimensions becomes a half turn through the held axis, and a size flip is the turn K. Nothing is resized.
6. **Read provenance.** Each corner in the new cell came from exactly one corner of the cube, along one circle. Name every new datum by that corner and circle.
7. **Check.** The turn must keep all 32 edges, four quarter turns must return every corner, and the Front string must land on the Back string as written.
8. **Choose each alignment by its behavior.** Where a number can be stated in more than one alignment, a scale, a scheme or a frame, carry each alignment to every observable it touches down-wind. Leave out any that misses a measurement by more than 2σ, and keep the best-matched (rule 9). A miss that every alignment shows, the standard one included, is inherited and stays on the record. The observables used to choose an alignment are not counted as evidence for it.
9. **Match every position.** For each point of interest, find the measurement at its instant and at each turn. Where none exists, record the empty cell and the measurement that would fill it. Compute the pull for every number the layer commits to, and keep misses visible. Before counting a misfit against the model, check its neighbours (4.9).

## 4.9 Finding a misfit by its neighbours

Rule 5 says that a datum that does not fit can be found from the data at the corners next to it, without the dynamics that made it.

1. **List the neighbours.** A corner has three neighbours along the cube's edges, one along w in the next cell, and the faces it shares. Take the measured data at each.
2. **Take a range from each.** The edge or face relation turns each neighbour's datum into a range for the misfit: its sign, cell, scale or rate. In the electrical frame the relations are identities, such as qφ = h, i = qω and v = φω.
3. **Intersect.** If the misfit lies inside every range, it sits at the wrong corner, cell, sign or resolution: move it. If not, check the neighbours themselves, their calibration and selection, before counting the misfit against the model.

## The four cells and the shared faces

One quarter turn J carries the cube into the next cell, and two give the inverse, J² = −1.
Neighbouring cells share one face: instant and rate share the Back face, rate and inverse the
turned Front face, inverse and anti-rate the turned Back face, and anti-rate and instant the
Front face. Four quarter turns return every corner.
