# Motor M1: from the given to the unknown

## Given: the classical, obvious information

Motor M1 is a 10 HP, 460 V, 60 Hz, three-phase NEMA Design B squirrel-cage induction motor. Its plate reads full-load speed 1775 rpm, full-load current 12.4 A, code letter G, NEMA Premium nominal efficiency 91.7 %, power factor 0.83, service factor 1.15, insulation class F. The NEC table current for a 10 HP 460 V motor is 14 A. The NEMA Premium table efficiency for 10 HP 4-pole is 91.7 %.

## Unknown, but implied: if the given is correct, these are correct too

Because the supply is 60 Hz and the full-load speed is 1775 rpm, the synchronous speed is 1800 rpm and the motor has 4 poles. Because the synchronous speed is 1800 rpm and the full-load speed is 1775 rpm, the full-load slip is about 1.4 %.

Because the output is 10 HP at 91.7 % efficiency, the input power at full load is about 8.1 kW. Because the input is about 8.1 kW at 460 V and 12.4 A, the full-load power factor is about 0.82, which agrees with the plate's 0.83. Because the output is 10 HP at 1775 rpm, the rated shaft torque is about 40 N·m.

Because the code letter is G, the locked-rotor current is between 70 A and 79 A, about 6.3 times full-load current. Because the table current is 14 A, the conductors must carry at least 17.5 A and the inverse-time breaker may be no larger than 35 A.

The no-load current is not on the plate; because this is a standard induction motor of this size, it is roughly 40 % of full-load current, about 5 A. The efficiency at full load, 91.7 %, is reached by three independent routes: the plate, the NEMA table, and the solved equivalent circuit.

## Two statements that should fall or stay open

The full-load slip is 5 %. The motor runs cool at full load.
