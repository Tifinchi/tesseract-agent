Nameplate, and the measured motor (IEEE 112 no-load and locked-rotor tests, three-phase)

The nameplate fields change what the tiles and checks compare against, and the nameplate current sets the overload. The test defaults are what the typical 10 HP, 460 V, 4-pole motor on this page would measure, so switching to "yes" with them reproduces the typical motor; enter your own motor's tests to make it exact.

The device type and its instantaneous multiple feed the 430.52 pairing and the inrush check. A clamp-meter or tachometer reading feeds the three-loads section: it returns the actual load three ways and can set the load slider.

Physics
The per-phase equivalent circuit of the induction motor (stator R₁ + jX₁, magnetizing jX_m ∥ R_c, rotor R₂′/s + jX₂′), solved for the slip that delivers the load; Thevenin pull-out; locked rotor with deep-bar rotor resistance and leakage saturation; negative-sequence and harmonic currents from the same circuit; three-phase voltage drop √3·I·L·(R cos θ + X sin θ); magnetizing saturation as a flux-dependent X_m; the circuit at the drive frequency with V/f held below base and voltage held above it, the affinity law for pump and fan loads; sag ride-through from pull-out torque at the sag voltage; DC offset as the circuit at zero frequency (current limited by R₁ alone, stationary field read by the standard DC-injection braking expression, flux bias on one half-cycle through the saturation curve); unbalance derate by the hottest phase; IEEE 112 parameter extraction from no-load and locked-rotor tests; the single-phase motor as two windings with forward and backward fields (symmetrical components of the main-winding current), the start capacitor chosen for maximum starting torque and the run capacitor for the least backward field; the DC machine as V = E + I_a R_a + brushes with E = Kω, shunt field constant, series field with the armature; the PM motor as E + I(R + jωL) with the current on the q axis below base speed and field weakening above it, the drive as a loss and a front end; load from a field reading by inverting the circuit on the measured current or speed; Kelvin's law for the conductor; first-order thermal model of the winding (R_th from the design rise, heating time constant by size) and of the overload relay.
Typical
Per-unit parameters scaled by size on the base P/0.78: R₁ = 0.040·HP^−0.33, R₂′ = 0.030·HP^−0.30, X₁ = X₂′ = 0.085 + 0.010·log₁₀HP, X_m = 1.3 + 0.9·log₁₀HP (capped at 4.0; ×1.15 for 2-pole, ×0.85 for 6-pole), core loss 0.034·HP^−0.17 of rated output, friction and windage 0.024·HP^−0.24 (2-pole ×(1.6 + 2.5/HP), 6-pole ×0.8), stray 1.8 % to 125 HP and 1.5 % to 500 HP (IEEE 112). At standstill R₂′ rises by 1.6 + 0.7·log₁₀HP and the leakage reactances fall to 80 % and 75 %. Saturation: magnetizing current × (1 + 0.25·φ⁶)/1.25 with φ the flux relative to rated; core-loss resistance scales as f^0.7; friction and windage as speed^2.5. Contactor dropout taken at under 75 % voltage for two cycles or more. Thermal: rated losses give an 80 K rise (class F insulation at class B rise, the usual premium design), heating time constant 15·HP^0.25 minutes, hot spot 10 K above the winding average. These follow the size trend of NEMA design B motors and were not fitted to the efficiency tables.

Three-phase and single-phase induction, DC brushed and brushless PM motors; wound-rotor, synchronous wound-field, universal and stepper motors are not modelled. NEC table currents are deliberately conservative: premium three-phase motors ordinarily run 5 to 15 % below Table 430.250, single-phase and DC motors 20 to 35 % below Tables 430.248 and 430.247. At rated V/f the efficiency does not depend on the voltage menu, which changes current, wire and drop. Not modelled: transients and surges, notching, bearing currents from a drive, load inertia during a sag, and the drive's own current limit at locked rotor.
