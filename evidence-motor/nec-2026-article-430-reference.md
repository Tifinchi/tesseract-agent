Which current to use (430.6(A)(1), page 70-386). For ordinary motors, the full-load current values in Tables 430.247 through 430.250 are used instead of the current marked on the motor nameplate when determining conductor ampacity, switch current ratings, and the ratings of branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection. Motors built for low speed (under 1200 rpm), high torque, canned pumps, and multispeed motors are the exceptions. The nameplate current is what the motor draws; the table current is what the code sizes the circuit from.

Code letters (Table 430.7(B), page 70-387). The locked-rotor indicating code letter gives locked-rotor kilovolt-amperes per horsepower: A 0 to 3.14; B 3.15 to 3.54; C 3.55 to 3.99; D 4.0 to 4.49; E 4.5 to 4.99; F 5.0 to 5.59; G 5.6 to 6.29; H 6.3 to 7.09; J 7.1 to 7.99; K 8.0 to 8.99; L 9.0 to 9.99; M 10.0 to 11.19; N 11.2 to 12.49; P 12.5 to 13.99; R 14.0 to 15.99; S 16.0 to 17.99; T 18.0 to 19.99; U 20.0 to 22.39; V 22.4 and up. Motors rated for both 50 and 60 Hz carry the code letter for 60 Hz.

Conductors for a single motor (430.22, page 70-390). Conductors supplying a single continuous-duty motor need an ampacity of at least 125 percent of the motor full-load current rating as determined by 430.6(A)(1), that is, of the table value. For a 10 hp 460 V three-phase motor that is 125 percent of 14 A, which is 17.5 A.

Thermal protector trip limits (430.32(A)(2), page 70-394). An integral thermal protector may trip at no more than 170 percent of the table full-load current for motors of 9 A or less, 156 percent from 9.1 A through 20 A, and 140 percent above 20 A.

Branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection (Table 430.52(C)(1), page 70-397). The maximum rating or setting, as a percentage of full-load current, by motor type: single-phase motors 300 percent for a nontime-delay fuse, 175 percent for a dual-element time-delay fuse, 800 percent for an instantaneous-trip breaker, 250 percent for an inverse-time breaker. AC polyphase motors other than wound-rotor: 300, 175, 800, 250. Squirrel cage motors other than Design B or C energy-efficient, and Design B or C premium efficiency: 300, 175, 800, 250. Design B energy-efficient, Design B premium efficiency, Design BE and Design CE: 300, 175, 1100, 250. Synchronous: 300, 175, 800, 250. Wound-rotor: 150, 150, 800, 150. Direct-current constant-voltage: 150, 150, 250, 150. The inverse-time breaker column also covers nonadjustable inverse-time breakers. Low-speed synchronous motors that start unloaded need no setting above 200 percent.

Where the table value will not start the motor (430.52(C)(1)(b), page 70-398). The rating of a time-delay dual-element fuse may be increased to at most 225 percent of full-load current. The rating of an inverse-time circuit breaker may be increased to at most 400 percent of full-load current when the full-load current is 100 A or less, and to at most 300 percent when it is over 100 A. For a 10 hp 460 V motor with a table current of 14 A, the base maximum for an inverse-time breaker is 250 percent, 35 A, and the ceiling is 400 percent, 56 A.

Instantaneous-trip breakers (430.52(C)(3), page 70-398). Permitted only as adjustable units in a listed combination motor controller with coordinated overload and short-circuit protection. Set no higher than the Table 430.52(C)(1) value; where that will not start the motor, up to 1300 percent of full-load current for motors other than Design B energy-efficient or premium efficiency, and up to 1700 percent for Design B or C energy-efficient or premium efficiency, Design BE and Design CE motors.

Highest rated motor (430.17, page 70-390). When a rule turns on the highest or smallest rated motor in a group, the rating is the table full-load current, not the nameplate current.
