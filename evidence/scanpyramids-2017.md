source: Morishima, K. et al. (2017). Discovery of a big void in Khufu's Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons. Nature 552, 386–390.
group: scanpyramids
status: compiled excerpt from memory of the paper; verify against the original before citing

Three independent muon detection techniques (nuclear emulsion films placed in the Queen's Chamber, scintillator hodoscopes in the Queen's Chamber, and gas detectors outside the pyramid) detected a large void above the Grand Gallery. The void is at least 30 m long, with a cross-section similar to that of the Grand Gallery (about 8 m high), located between 50 and 70 m above ground. Its muon signal is comparable to that of the Grand Gallery, consistent with an empty (air-filled) space. Muon imaging records only material above the detector positions; the bedrock beneath the pyramid is outside the method's view from these positions. A later campaign (2023) confirmed the void and imaged a 9 m corridor behind the north face chevrons.
