Petrie's survey of the Great Pyramid gives the mean length of the base sides as 9068.8 inches (230.35 m), with the four sides differing by at most a few inches. The original height, reconstructed from the casing angle and the base, is 5776 inches ± 7 (146.71 m). The casing angle is 51° 52' ± 2'. The sides are oriented to true north within about 3 to 4 arcminutes.

The King's Chamber measures 412.6 inches long by 206.3 inches wide (10.48 m by 5.24 m), with a height of about 230.1 inches (5.84 m); the walls are granite in five courses. Its floor lies at 1691.4 to 1693.7 inches above the pavement (42.96 to 43.02 m). The chamber is roofed by nine granite beams.

The Queen's Chamber floor lies at about 834.4 inches (21.19 m) above the pavement. The chamber is 226.5 inches long by 205.7 inches wide (5.75 m by 5.22 m), with a gabled roof, and is placed on the central axis of the pyramid, east to west.

The coffer in the King's Chamber is 89.62 inches long, 38.50 inches wide and 41.31 inches high outside (2.276 m by 0.978 m by 1.049 m). It has a groove for a sliding lid along the top of three sides and three pin holes in the rim at the west end; the lid is absent. The coffer is wider than the Ascending Passage, so it was placed in the chamber during construction. Its surfaces show the marks of a saw and a tubular drill.

A worked block of granite was found lying in the Descending Passage, worked on both faces and one end; its origin is a puzzle.
