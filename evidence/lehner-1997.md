Great Pyramid of Khufu: base 230.33 m, original height 146.59 m, slope 51° 50' 40". Volume about 2.6 million cubic metres; roughly 2.3 million blocks. The King's Chamber, lined and roofed in granite, measures about 10.47 m by 5.23 m and is 5.84 m high; above it are five relieving chambers roofed with granite beams, the topmost gabled. The Queen's Chamber lies on the pyramid's central axis and has a corbelled niche in its east wall. The Grand Gallery is 47 m long and 8.6 m high. The chamber floors lie at roughly 43 m (King's) and 21 m (Queen's) above the base.

Khafre's pyramid: base 215 m, height 143.5 m, slope 53° 10', built on ground about 10 m higher than Khufu's. Menkaure's pyramid: base about 105 m, height about 65 m, the lower courses cased in granite.

The pyramid complex as a whole was a machine for the king's resurrection: valley temple, causeway, mortuary temple, pyramid and boat pits. Lehner argues that the organization needed to build the pyramids, rotating labor, provisioning and administration, did as much to build the Egyptian state as the state did to build the pyramids.
