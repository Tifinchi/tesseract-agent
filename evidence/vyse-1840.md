Vyse blasted upward from Davison's Chamber in 1837 and opened four further relieving chambers above the King's Chamber (named for Wellington, Nelson, Lady Arbuthnot and Campbell), the topmost gabled. In these sealed chambers he found quarry marks in red ochre, including cartouches of Khufu and the names of work gangs. Some of the marks run under the joints between blocks, showing they were painted before the blocks were set. Vyse also had the pit in the floor of the Subterranean Chamber deepened to about 11 m and excavated beneath the coffer in the King's Chamber, finding solid masonry and rock in both cases. The lowest ceiling beams of the King's Chamber are cracked and had been plastered over in antiquity.
