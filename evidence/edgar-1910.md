The Grotto is a natural cavity or fissure in the rock uncovered when the builders levelled the site; the well shaft passes through it, and the builders walled it with small courses of stone and packed it with sand and gravel as foundation fill. Investigators after Al Mamoun's time broke through the lower courses of its south wall, found only sand and gravel, and threw their spoil down the well, blocking its lower end. Wedged at the east edge of the deep hollow in the Grotto's floor lies a large granite stone of broken appearance, apparently a fragment of a larger block, with two worked surfaces at right angles to each other and parts of two large holes drilled through it.

The large granite block found by Professor Flinders Petrie in the Descending Passage has a four-inch drill hole through it; the two next largest granite pieces also each have a pair of drill holes. The block lacks the projections and rebates of the Granite Leaf; Petrie's suggestion that it was a further portcullis from the Antechamber faces the objection that the grooves there are too wide. Its original purpose is unknown.

The well shaft descends about 125 feet below the Grotto; its inclined section is about 30 inches high and 22 to 25 inches wide, with rough footholds cut in the walls.
